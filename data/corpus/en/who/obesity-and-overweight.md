# Obesity and overweight

**Last updated:** 8 December 2025

## Key facts

- In 2022, 1 in 8 people globally lived with obesity.
- Adult obesity has more than doubled since 1990; adolescent obesity has quadrupled.
- 2.5 billion adults were overweight in 2022, with 890 million living with obesity.
- 43% of adults aged 18+ were overweight; 16% lived with obesity.
- 35 million children under 5 were overweight in 2024.
- Over 390 million children and adolescents aged 5–19 were overweight in 2022, including 160 million with obesity.

## Overview

Overweight involves excessive fat deposits. WHO classifies obesity as a chronic, relapsing disease arising from complex interactions between genetics, neurobiology, eating behaviours, access to healthy diet, market forces, and the broader environment.

Diagnosis uses Body Mass Index (BMI): weight (kg)/height² (m²).

## Definition

### Adults

- **Overweight:** BMI ≥ 25
- **Obesity:** BMI ≥ 30

### Children Aged 5–19 Years

- **Overweight:** BMI-for-age > 1 standard deviation above WHO Growth Reference median
- **Obesity:** > 2 standard deviations above WHO Growth Reference median

### Children Under 5 Years

- **Overweight:** Weight-for-height > 2 standard deviations above WHO Child Growth Standards median
- **Obesity:** Weight-for-height > 3 standard deviations above median

## Causes

Overweight and obesity result from imbalanced energy intake versus expenditure. Obesity typically involves multiple factors including environmental and psychosocial influences and genetic variants.

Key environmental barriers include limited access to affordable, healthy food and spaces for physical activity, plus inadequate health system responses to early weight gain detection.

## Health consequences

Higher-than-optimal BMI caused an estimated 3.7 million deaths from noncommunicable diseases in 2021, including cardiovascular disease, diabetes, cancers, neurological disorders, chronic respiratory disease, and digestive disorders.

Childhood obesity increases risk for type 2 diabetes and cardiovascular disease, with adverse psychosocial effects including bullying and reduced school performance.

Predicted global obesity costs reach US$ 3 trillion annually by 2030 and over US$ 18 trillion by 2060.

## Prevention and management

### Individual-level interventions

- Ensure appropriate pregnancy weight gain
- Practice exclusive breastfeeding for 6 months, continuing to 24 months or beyond
- Support healthy eating, physical activity, limited screen time
- Limit sugar-sweetened beverages and energy-dense foods
- Increase fruit, vegetables, legumes, whole grains, nuts
- Engage in regular physical activity

### Health practitioner responsibilities

- Assess weight and height
- Provide healthy diet and lifestyle counselling
- Offer management options including therapeutic and surgical measures when appropriate
- Monitor other NCD risk factors and comorbidities

### Societal approaches

Obesity requires multisectoral action addressing food manufacturing, marketing, pricing, poverty reduction, and urban planning.

## GLP-1 therapies (December 2025 update)

In December 2025, WHO published guidelines on glucagon-like peptide-1 (GLP-1) therapies for adult obesity treatment, supporting safe, equitable and appropriate inclusion of pharmacological therapy within comprehensive chronic care programs.

## WHO response

WHO endorsed Global Nutrition Targets aiming to prevent childhood overweight increases and halt diabetes/obesity rise by 2030. The 2022 World Health Assembly adopted the WHO Acceleration Plan to Stop Obesity.
