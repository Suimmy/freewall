# Healthy diet

## Key facts

- Diet fundamentally influences individual and population health; unhealthy eating patterns represent a significant disease risk factor.
- Nutritious diets protect against malnutrition and noncommunicable diseases including diabetes, cardiovascular disease, stroke, and cancer.
- Four foundational principles should guide any healthy diet: adequacy, balance, moderation and diversity.
- Minimally processed foods low in unhealthy fats, added sugars, and sodium form the basis of healthy eating.

## Carbohydrates

Carbohydrates provide primary energy. Unrefined sources should comprise approximately 45–75% of total daily energy.

**Recommendations:**
- Choose whole grains (unprocessed maize, millet, oats, wheat, brown rice)
- Include pulses: lentils, chickpeas, beans, dried peas
- Fresh, frozen, or canned fruits and vegetables without added sugars or excess sodium
- Limit fruit juices despite natural origins due to free sugar content

**Fiber and produce intake:**
- Adults and children 10+: minimum 400 grams of fruits and vegetables per day
- Children 2-5 years: at least 250 grams
- Children 6-9 years: at least 350 grams
- Daily dietary fiber: minimum 25 grams for older children and adults

## Sugars

- Limit free sugars to less than 10% of total daily energy intake (approximately 50 grams for 2000-calorie diets)
- Further reduction to 5% or less provides additional health benefits
- Avoid non-sugar sweeteners as sugar-reduction strategy
- Free sugars include manufacturer-added and consumer-added sugars plus naturally occurring sugars in honey, syrups, and fruit juices

## Fats

Dietary fat is essential; two fatty acids cannot be synthesized and must come from food. Adults should consume minimum 15% of the energy consumed per day from fat.

**Quality matters:**
- Unsaturated fats (fish, avocado, nuts, plant oils) are preferable
- Limit saturated fat to no more than 10% of total energy intake
- Eliminate trans fats; limit to maximum 1% of total energy
- Replace saturated fats with polyunsaturated or monounsaturated options

## Protein

Adults need 10–15% of total daily energy intake (approximately 50-75 grams for 2000-calorie diets).

- Combine animal and plant protein sources
- Consider shifting toward plant-based proteins in some contexts to reduce noncommunicable disease risk
- Animal proteins remain important for children and pregnant/lactating women

## Salt/sodium and potassium

High sodium intake increases blood pressure and cardiovascular disease risk. Potassium mitigates these negative effects.

**Sodium recommendations:**
- Adults: limit to less than 5 grams per day (2 grams per day sodium)
- Most salt comes from processed foods, bread, and condiments

**Potassium:**
- Target minimum 90 mmol/day (3510 mg/day) for adults
- Increase through fresh fruits and vegetables

## Vitamins and minerals (micronutrients)

Approximately 30 essential micronutrients (13 vitamins, 16 minerals) prevent serious health issues.

**Strategies:**
- Consume diverse nutrient-dense foods: fruits, vegetables, whole grains, legumes, nuts, seeds, lean proteins
- Prioritize micronutrient-rich sources (beans for iron, leafy greens for vitamin A)
- Implement food fortification programs in countries with high deficiency prevalence (20%+)

## For infants and young children

Optimal nutrition in the first two years supports growth and cognitive development while reducing later obesity and disease risk.

**Key recommendations:**
- Exclusive breastfeeding for first 6 months
- Continue breastfeeding until 2+ years
- From 6 months: introduce diverse, safe, nutrient-dense complementary foods without added salt or sugars
- Include animal source foods and diverse vegetables
- Avoid sugary, salty, trans fat-containing foods and sweet beverages
