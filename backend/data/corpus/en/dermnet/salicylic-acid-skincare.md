# Salicylic Acid in Skincare: A Medical Fact Sheet

## Overview

Salicylic acid is a keratolytic medicine used to treat scaly skin conditions including viral warts, psoriasis, seborrheic dermatitis, atopic dermatitis, ichthyosis, and acne. It works by softening keratin to loosen dry, flaky skin and improve penetration of additional treatments.

## What salicylic acid does in skincare

Salicylic acid works by softening keratin, a protein that forms part of the skin structure. In acne specifically, it slows cell shedding inside follicles to prevent clogging and helps break down comedones. Combined with other medicines, it removes the upper skin layer for better absorption.

## Concentration ranges and professional formulations

Salicylic acid preparations range from 0.5% up to 30%. Over-the-counter options include 0.5% cleansers and 2-3% lotions. Professional-strength formulations reach 15-27% in paints and gels for warts and corns. Prescription preparations combine salicylic acid with topical corticosteroids for enhanced efficacy.

## Side effects and warnings

Mild stinging may occur especially on broken skin and when higher concentrations are used. More serious concerns include moderate to severe irritation, flushing, and unusual warmth. Salicylic acid poisoning (overdose) with topical preparations is rare, but symptoms include confusion, nausea, vomiting, dizziness, and rapid breathing. Avoid contact with eyes and mucous membranes.

## DIY salicylic acid from aspirin

Authoritative dermatology resources provide no guidance on creating salicylic acid from aspirin or other home remedies. Aspirin is acetylsalicylic acid — chemically related to but not equivalent to topical salicylic acid in pharmaceutical skincare formulations. No instructions, safety protocols, or formulation advice for DIY preparations appear in professional medical references.

## Why DIY skincare is risky

It is very important that salicylic acid preparations be used strictly as directed on the label. Improper use increases skin absorption and poisoning risk. Homemade formulations lack pharmaceutical standards for purity, concentration accuracy, and stabilization. Salicylic acid should not be used on irritated skin or on any area that is infected or reddened. Mixing aspirin tablets with creams or milk for application to dark spots, wrinkles, or red marks is not a recognized dermatological protocol and may cause irritation, burns, or worsened skin conditions.

## When to see a dermatologist

Consult a dermatologist for moderate or severe skin irritation, unexpected reactions, or if pregnant or breastfeeding. Children under 2 years should not use salicylic acid without medical monitoring. Professional guidance ensures appropriate concentration selection and application safety for specific conditions.
