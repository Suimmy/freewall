# Diabetes

## Key facts

- The number of people with diabetes increased from 200 million in 1990 to 830 million in 2022, with faster growth in low- and middle-income countries.
- More than half of people living with diabetes did not take medication for their diabetes in 2022.
- Diabetes causes blindness, kidney failure, heart attacks, stroke and lower limb amputation.
- In 2021, diabetes and related kidney disease caused over 2 million deaths, with high blood glucose responsible for approximately 11% of cardiovascular deaths.
- Prevention strategies include healthy diet, regular physical activity, maintaining normal body weight, and avoiding tobacco.
- Diabetes can be managed through diet, physical activity, medication, and regular screening for complications.

## Overview

Diabetes is a chronic condition occurring when the pancreas does not produce enough insulin or when the body cannot effectively use the insulin it produces. Insulin regulates blood glucose levels.

In 2022, 14% of adults aged 18+ had diabetes, compared to 7% in 1990. More than half of adults with diabetes weren't taking medication, with lowest coverage in low- and middle-income countries.

Diabetes directly caused 1.6 million deaths in 2021, with 47% occurring before age 70. An additional 530,000 deaths resulted from diabetes-related kidney disease.

## Symptoms

Diabetes symptoms may develop suddenly or gradually:

- Excessive thirst
- Frequent urination
- Blurred vision
- Fatigue
- Unintentional weight loss

Long-term complications include damage to blood vessels affecting the heart, eyes, kidneys, and nerves, increasing risks of heart attack, stroke, kidney failure, vision loss, and foot ulcers.

## Type 1 Diabetes

Type 1 involves insufficient insulin production requiring daily injections. In 2017, approximately 9 million people had type 1 diabetes, predominantly in high-income countries. Neither its cause nor the means to prevent it are known.

## Type 2 Diabetes

Type 2 impairs how the body uses glucose for energy. Contributing factors include being overweight, insufficient exercise, and genetics. More than 95% of people with diabetes have type 2 diabetes.

Early diagnosis through regular check-ups and blood tests is crucial for preventing serious complications.

## Gestational Diabetes

Gestational diabetes involves elevated blood glucose during pregnancy. Women with gestational diabetes face increased pregnancy complications and elevated future type 2 diabetes risk for themselves and their children.

## Prevention

Lifestyle modifications are optimal for preventing or delaying type 2 diabetes:

- Maintain healthy body weight
- Engage in at least 150 minutes of moderate weekly exercise
- Consume a healthy diet low in sugar and saturated fat
- Avoid tobacco use

## Diagnosis and Treatment

Early diagnosis uses relatively inexpensive blood glucose testing. Type 1 requires insulin injections for survival.

Management emphasizes maintaining healthy lifestyle habits. Type 2 patients may require medications including:

- Metformin
- Sulfonylureas
- Sodium-glucose co-transporters type 2 (SGLT-2) inhibitors

Additional medications address blood pressure and cardiovascular risk. Medical care may include foot care, kidney disease screening, and eye exams for retinopathy.
