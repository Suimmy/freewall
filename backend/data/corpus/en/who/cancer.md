# Cancer

## Overview

Cancer is a broad classification of illnesses affecting any body part, characterized by the rapid development of abnormal cells that expand past their normal limits and may invade adjacent tissues or spread to distant organs — a process called metastasis, which represents the primary cause of cancer-related death.

## The problem

Cancer constitutes a major global mortality cause, responsible for nearly 10 million deaths in 2022. The most frequently diagnosed cancers included:

- Lung (2.5 million cases)
- Breast (2.3 million cases)
- Colon and rectum (1.9 million cases)
- Prostate (1.5 million cases)
- Skin non-melanoma (1.2 million cases)
- Stomach (1.0 million cases)

Leading causes of cancer mortality in 2022 were lung (1.82 million deaths), colorectal (904,000 deaths), liver (760,000 deaths), breast (666,000 deaths), and stomach (660,000 deaths).

## Causes

Cancer develops through multistage transformation of normal cells into tumor cells, involving genetic factors and three external agent categories:

- **Physical carcinogens**: ultraviolet and ionizing radiation
- **Chemical carcinogens**: asbestos, tobacco smoke components, alcohol, aflatoxin, arsenic
- **Biological carcinogens**: infections from certain viruses, bacteria, parasites

Cancer incidence escalates with age due to cumulative exposure to risk factors and diminished cellular repair mechanisms.

## Risk factors

Tobacco use, alcohol consumption, unhealthy diet, physical inactivity, and air pollution elevate cancer risk. Approximately 10% of cancers diagnosed in 2022 globally were attributed to carcinogenic infections, including Helicobacter pylori, human papillomavirus (HPV), hepatitis B virus, hepatitis C virus, and Epstein-Barr virus.

## Reducing the burden

Approximately 38% of cancers can currently be prevented by avoiding risk factors and implementing existing evidence-based prevention strategies. Cancer burden reduction also occurs through early detection and appropriate patient treatment. Many cancers demonstrate high cure rates when diagnosed early and treated appropriately.

## Prevention

Cancer risk reduction involves:

- Avoiding tobacco use
- Maintaining healthy body weight
- Consuming nutritious diets with adequate fruits and vegetables
- Engaging in regular physical activity
- Limiting alcohol consumption
- Obtaining HPV and hepatitis B vaccinations when recommended
- Avoiding ultraviolet radiation exposure and employing sun protection
- Ensuring safe radiation use in healthcare
- Reducing outdoor and indoor air pollution exposure, including radon

## Early detection

Cancer mortality decreases when cases receive timely detection and treatment.

### Early diagnosis

Early cancer identification increases treatment responsiveness, survival probability, and reduces morbidity and costs.

### Screening

Screening identifies individuals with findings suggesting specific cancers or precancerous conditions before symptom development. Screening programs suit certain cancer types but require special equipment and dedicated personnel. Examples include:

- HPV testing as preferred cervical cancer screening modality
- Mammography for breast cancer screening in women aged 50–69 with strong health systems

## Treatment

Accurate cancer diagnosis proves essential for appropriate treatment, as each type requires specific regimens. Treatment typically involves surgery, radiotherapy, and/or systemic therapy (chemotherapy, hormonal treatments, targeted biological therapies).

Several common cancers — including breast, cervical, oral, and colorectal — demonstrate high cure probabilities when detected early and treated according to established best practices. Testicular seminoma and various childhood leukemias and lymphomas also show high cure rates with appropriate treatment.

## Palliative care

Palliative care relieves symptoms and suffering rather than providing cure, improving quality of life for patients and families. Relief from physical, psychosocial, and spiritual problems through palliative care is possible for more than 90% of patients with advanced stages of cancer.

Improved oral morphine access is strongly recommended for treating moderate-to-severe cancer pain, experienced by over 80% of terminally ill cancer patients.
