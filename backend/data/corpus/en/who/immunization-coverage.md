# Immunization coverage

**15 July 2025**

## Key facts

- Globally in 2024, there were 14.3 million children missing out on any vaccination – so-called zero-dose children.
- DTP3 vaccine coverage reached 85% in 2024.
- Measles first-dose vaccination stood at 84% in 2024, below 2019's 86% level.
- HPV vaccine coverage in girls increased from 27% (2023) to 31% (2024).
- Yellow fever vaccine coverage in at-risk countries remains at 52%, below the recommended 80%.

## Overview

While immunization represents one of the most successful public health interventions, progress toward Immunization Agenda 2030 targets shows concerning patterns. Approximately 85% of infants received the complete DTP3 series in 2024, though substantial disparities exist between high and low-income nations.

Measles serves as an early warning system for immunity gaps. In 2024, 20.6 million children missed their routine first dose of measles, compared to 19.3 million in 2019 — a troubling reversal of progress.

## Global immunization coverage 2024

- **Haemophilus influenzae type b (Hib):** 78% global coverage with regional variation (93% in Europe; 34% in Western Pacific).
- **Hepatitis B:** 84% global coverage for 3-dose series; 45% coverage for birth-dose vaccination.
- **Human papillomavirus (HPV):** 31% first-dose coverage among girls; 147 countries now offer HPV in programs.
- **Malaria:** New vaccine implementations underway across African nations as part of integrated control efforts.
- **Measles:** 84% received first dose; 76% received second dose by age 2.
- **Meningitis:** MenAfriVac reached 410 million people; no confirmed NmA cases since 2017.
- **Polio:** 84% received 3 doses globally; limited transmission continues in Afghanistan and Pakistan.
- **Rotavirus:** Introduced in 131 countries; 59% global coverage.
- **Rubella:** 73% global coverage in 178 Member States.
- **Yellow fever:** 38 of 40 at-risk countries included vaccine; 52% coverage achieved.

## Key challenges

14.3 million infants did not receive an initial dose of DTP vaccine, with additional 5.6 million partially vaccinated. Approximately 55% reside in ten countries: Afghanistan, Democratic Republic of Congo, Ethiopia, India, Indonesia, Nigeria, Pakistan, Philippines, Sudan, and Yemen.

Subnational monitoring data remains critical for tailored vaccination strategies addressing coverage gaps.

## WHO response

**Immunization Agenda 2030** provides framework for 2021–2030 global vaccination strategy, operationalized through regional and national mechanisms.

**Cervical cancer elimination** strategy prioritizes HPV vaccine introduction in all countries, targeting 90% coverage by 2030.
