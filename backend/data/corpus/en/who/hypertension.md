# Hypertension (High Blood Pressure)

## Key facts

An estimated 1.4 billion adults aged 30-79 years worldwide had hypertension in 2024, representing 33% of the population in this age range. Two-thirds live in low- and middle-income countries. Approximately 600 million adults are unaware of their condition, while 630 million are diagnosed and treated, and only 320 million have it under control. Earlier WHO communications cited 1.28 billion as the global hypertension figure; the 2024 update is 1.4 billion.

## Overview

Hypertension (high blood pressure) occurs when pressure in blood vessels is 140/90 mmHg or higher. Most people experience no symptoms, making regular blood pressure checks essential. Risk factors include older age, genetics, obesity, physical inactivity, high-salt diets, and excessive alcohol consumption.

## Symptoms

Most people with hypertension experience no symptoms. Very high blood pressure (usually 180/120 mmHg or higher) can cause severe headaches, chest pain, dizziness, difficulty breathing, nausea, vomiting, and blurred vision among other complications. The only reliable detection method is professional blood pressure measurement.

## Risk factors

Modifiable risk factors include unhealthy diets (excessive salt, saturated and trans fats, insufficient fruits and vegetables), physical inactivity, tobacco and alcohol consumption, and being overweight or obese. Air pollution represents the most significant environmental risk factor. Non-modifiable factors include family history, age over 65, and conditions like diabetes or kidney disease.

## Complications

Uncontrolled hypertension damages the heart by hardening arteries and reducing blood and oxygen flow, causing angina, heart attack, heart failure, and irregular heartbeat. It can burst or block arteries supplying the brain, causing stroke, and can damage kidneys, leading to kidney failure.

## Diagnosis and treatment

Lifestyle modifications include eating a healthy, low-salt diet, losing weight, increasing physical activity, and quitting tobacco. Common medications include ACE inhibitors, angiotensin-2 receptor blockers, calcium channel blockers, and diuretics. Blood pressure goals are less than 130/80 mmHg for those with cardiovascular disease, diabetes, or chronic kidney disease; otherwise less than 140/90 mmHg.

## Prevention

Lifestyle changes can prevent and lower high blood pressure. Recommendations include eating more vegetables and fruits, sitting less, performing at least 150 minutes per week of moderate-intensity aerobic activity or 75 minutes per week of vigorous aerobic activity, and doing strength-building exercises twice weekly. Consume under 2 grams of sodium (under 5 grams of salt) daily, avoid foods high in saturated or trans fats, avoid tobacco and excessive alcohol, and manage stress while regularly monitoring blood pressure.

## WHO response

WHO released updated pharmacological treatment guidelines for hypertension in adults in 2021. The organization supports the Global Hearts Initiative, launched with the U.S. CDC in 2016, which includes the HEARTS technical package addressing counseling, evidence-based protocols, medication access, risk management, team-based care, and monitoring systems. Since 2017, implementation across 40+ low- and middle-income countries has placed 13.5 million people on protocol-based hypertension treatment.
