# Reserpine (Rauwolfia Alkaloid): Drug Information and Safety

## Overview

Reserpine is a rauwolfia alkaloid medication formerly used to treat hypertension and severe agitation. Reserpine is no longer available in the United States. The compound is the principal active alkaloid in Rauwolfia serpentina (Thai: ระย่อม), a plant historically used in traditional medicine. Patients currently taking it should consult their doctor about alternative treatments.

## Why prescribed

Reserpine addressed high blood pressure and severe mental agitation. Untreated hypertension damages vital organs, causing heart disease, stroke, kidney failure, and vision loss. Lifestyle modifications including low-salt diet, exercise, and weight management complemented pharmacological treatment.

## Mechanism

The medication works by slowing the activity of the nervous system, causing the heartbeat to slow and the blood vessels to relax. This mechanism reduces cardiovascular workload but carries psychiatric risks not present with modern alternatives.

## Serious side effects

Seek immediate medical attention for: depression, nightmares, fainting, slow heartbeat, chest pain, and swollen extremities. These complications warrant emergency evaluation and medication discontinuation.

## Depression and suicide risk warning

Patients should tell their doctor if they have or have ever had a history of depression. This medication significantly increases depression and nightmare risk, making it unsuitable for vulnerable patients regardless of blood pressure control benefits. This is the primary reason reserpine has been discontinued in many markets in favor of safer antihypertensives.

## Why raw Rauwolfia plant use is unsafe

Self-medication with raw Rauwolfia root preparations carries the same psychiatric risks as pharmaceutical reserpine plus uncontrolled dosing. Traditional uses for fever, parasites, urination, or menstruation are not supported by modern clinical evidence and expose patients to serious side effects without medical monitoring.

## Special precautions

Disclose all medications, allergies, kidney disease, gallstones, ulcers, ulcerative colitis, and depression history. Pregnancy exposure requires immediate physician notification. Older adults should avoid reserpine due to safer alternatives. Avoid alcohol and machinery operation due to drowsiness and dizziness.

## Drug interactions

Doctors may need to change the doses of medications or monitor patients carefully for side effects when combining reserpine with other prescription drugs, supplements, or herbal products. Comprehensive medication disclosure is essential.
