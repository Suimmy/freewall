# Multivitamin/mineral supplements

## What are multivitamin/mineral (MVM) dietary supplements?

Multivitamin/mineral supplements contain a combination of vitamins and minerals, and sometimes other ingredients. Each vitamin and mineral has a unique role in the body.

Important note: MVMs cannot take the place of eating a variety of foods that are important to a healthy diet. Foods provide fiber and other beneficial compounds beyond vitamins and minerals.

## What kinds are available?

Companies choose which vitamins and minerals — and amounts — to include. There is no standard MVM formulation. Common types include:

- Basic, once-daily products with vitamins and minerals in recommended amounts
- Products with higher-than-recommended amounts in multiple daily pills
- Specialized MVMs marketed for athletic performance, energy, weight management, immunity, or eye health (often containing herbal ingredients)

Products use Daily Value (DV) percentages on labels, which often differ from Recommended Dietary Allowances (RDAs) or Adequate Intakes (AIs).

## Effects on health

### Increase nutrient intakes

Taking an MVM increases daily nutrient amounts, helping people meet recommendations when dietary intake is insufficient. However, MVMs can raise intake above safe upper limits for nutrients like iron, vitamin A, zinc, niacin, and folic acid.

### Health promotion and chronic disease prevention

Research shows mixed results because studies use different products and compositions change frequently. Most studies indicate MVMs have little or no effect on chronic disease outcomes.

### Cancer

Evidence is unclear. One study found male physicians taking basic MVMs for ~11 years had slightly reduced cancer development risk (not mortality). Results vary for breast, colon, and prostate cancers across studies.

### Cardiovascular disease

Most studies in men and women comparing MVMs to a placebo have found that the MVMs do not reduce the risk of CVD, including heart attacks or strokes or resulting deaths.

### Cataracts and age-related macular degeneration

The Age-Related Eye Disease Study (AREDS) demonstrated that high-dose supplements containing vitamin C (500 mg), vitamin E (400 IU), beta-carotene (15 mg), zinc (80 mg), and copper (2 mg) slowed AMD vision loss in affected individuals over 6 years.

### Pregnancy and birth outcomes

Prenatal MVMs help pregnant women obtain adequate nutrients, particularly folic acid and iron.

### Other outcomes

Most studies found no MVM effect on osteoporosis, lung disease, psychiatric disorders, or mortality. Some research suggests older adults taking MVMs for 1–3 years showed improved cognitive function and memory.

## Should I take a multivitamin/mineral?

Consider MVMs if you:

- Don't obtain sufficient vitamins and minerals from food alone
- Follow low-calorie diets or have poor appetite
- Avoid certain foods (strict vegetarians/vegans)
- Have specific medical conditions

### Specific recommendations

- **Women of childbearing age:** 400 mcg/day folic acid from fortified foods or supplements reduces birth defect risk
- **Pregnant women:** Daily prenatal MVM provides folic acid, iron, iodine, and vitamin D
- **Breastfed infants:** Vitamin D supplements (10 mcg/400 IU/day) if mother is B12-deficient or vegan
- **Age 50+:** Obtain vitamin B12 from fortified foods or supplements, as body absorption of naturally-occurring B12 declines

## Can MVMs be harmful?

Basic MVMs are generally safe. However, avoid exceeding upper limits when combining MVMs with fortified foods and other supplements.

**Important warnings:**

- Smokers and former smokers should avoid MVMs containing large amounts of beta-carotene and vitamin A, which may increase lung cancer risk
- Excessive vitamin A during pregnancy increases birth defect risk (beta-carotene does not pose this risk)

## Drug interactions

Basic MVMs rarely interact with medications. **Critical exception:** If taking warfarin (Coumadin, Jantoven) for blood clotting, consult your healthcare provider before taking any MVM or supplement containing vitamin K, as it reduces drug effectiveness.

## MVMs and healthful eating

People should get most of their nutrients from food and beverages, according to the federal government's Dietary Guidelines for Americans. Foods contain vitamins, minerals, fiber, and other health-promoting compounds. Fortified foods and supplements are useful when meeting specific nutrient needs through diet alone is impossible (such as during pregnancy).
