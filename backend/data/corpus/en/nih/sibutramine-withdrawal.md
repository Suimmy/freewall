# Sibutramine (Meridia / Reductil): Withdrawal and Safety Profile

## Overview

Sibutramine is an appetite suppressant previously marketed as Meridia and Reductil for weight management. The drug was withdrawn globally due to serious cardiovascular safety concerns identified during clinical trials and post-marketing surveillance.

## Background and approval history

Sibutramine was approved by the FDA and marketed internationally under brand names including Meridia in the United States and Reductil in Europe. The medication was indicated for weight loss in obese patients and became widely prescribed during the 1990s and 2000s.

## Reason for withdrawal

The withdrawal followed discovery of significant cardiovascular risks during the SCOUT trial, which demonstrated increased rates of heart attack, stroke, and cardiovascular death in patients taking sibutramine compared to placebo.

## SCOUT trial cardiovascular findings

The SCOUT (Sibutramine Cardiovascular Outcomes Trial) revealed that sibutramine users experienced elevated rates of serious cardiovascular events. The trial demonstrated increased cardiovascular risks in the treatment group, prompting regulatory action across multiple jurisdictions.

## Hepatotoxicity

While hepatotoxicity was not the primary reason for withdrawal, sibutramine has been documented to cause liver injury in some patients, ranging from mild transaminitis to severe hepatitis requiring monitoring during therapy.

## Withdrawal timeline

The European Medicines Agency suspended sibutramine in January 2010. The U.S. FDA withdrew approval in October 2010 due to the cardiovascular safety profile demonstrated in clinical evidence and ongoing safety reviews.

## Counterfeit and grey market concerns

Following official withdrawal, sibutramine remains available through illegal online pharmacies and grey market channels — including products marketed under names like "Reduce 15" — presenting additional risks to consumers purchasing unapproved or counterfeit products without medical supervision.
